# Phase 2 — Cai mapping to Guo

This phase evaluates how strongly the published Guo *Sapria himalayana* genome is supported by independent sequencing data from the Cai accession, and uses those data to construct a controlled Cai-on-Guo pseudogenome for downstream comparison.

Rather than treating the large difference between the published Cai and Guo assemblies as direct biological divergence, this phase asks how much of the apparent difference is supported by independent Cai reads and how much may reflect genome reconstruction.

## Main questions

1. How much of the published Guo assembly is supported by Cai sequencing reads?
2. Which small sequence differences are strongly supported between Cai and Guo?
3. Which larger structural discrepancies are detected by Cai long reads?
4. Can a Guo-backbone genome carrying confident Cai fixed alleles provide a controlled representation for separating sequence divergence from assembly/reconstruction effects?

## Input datasets

The analyses in this phase use the published Guo assembly as the reference genome and independent Cai sequencing reads as query evidence.

Cai evidence includes:

- Oxford Nanopore long reads
- Illumina short reads

The Guo assembly is therefore not treated as unquestioned ground truth. It is used as a common coordinate system against which independent Cai support and sequence differences are evaluated.

## Cai read support for the Guo assembly

### Oxford Nanopore reads

Cai ONT reads mapped extensively to the Guo assembly:

- primary mapping rate: **92.68%**
- reference breadth covered: **88.11%**
- mean depth: approximately **14.4×**
- approximately **1.816 Gb** of the Guo assembly had Cai ONT support
- approximately **245 Mb** lacked ONT coverage
- **9,204** Guo scaffolds had at least 90% breadth support
- **1,402** scaffolds had zero ONT coverage

### Illumina reads

Cai Illumina reads showed even broader support:

- primary mapping rate: **97.87%**
- reference breadth covered: approximately **92.93%**
- mean depth: approximately **41.94×**
- approximately **1.70 Gb** of Guo sequence occurred in scaffolds with at least 90% breadth support
- only approximately **0.56 Mb** occurred in completely unsupported scaffolds

Together, these results indicate that a large fraction of the Guo representation is independently recognized by Cai sequencing data, despite the substantial difference in published assembly size.

## High-confidence small variants

Strictly filtered Cai-versus-Guo small variants total:

- **4,535,955** high-confidence variants
- **3,704,632** SNPs
- **831,323** indels
- **3,377,317** heterozygous-like calls
- **1,158,638** homozygous-alternate calls

Basic filtering required:

- QUAL ≥ 30
- GQ ≥ 20
- DP between 10 and 100

The stricter call set additionally incorporated allele-balance criteria for heterozygous-like calls and a minimum alternate-allele fraction of 0.80 for homozygous-alternate calls.

These variants represent sequence-level differences supported by Cai Illumina data, but should not be interpreted as population-level variation because only two accessions are involved.

## Structural discrepancies

Cai ONT reads produced **54,117 PASS structural-discrepancy calls** with Sniffles2.

These events are treated conservatively as read-supported structural discrepancies between the Cai sequencing data and the Guo assembly coordinate system. They are not automatically interpreted as biological structural variants because assembly and reconstruction differences can contribute to the observed signal.

## Cai-on-Guo fixed pseudogenome

A fixed Cai-on-Guo pseudogenome was generated by applying confident homozygous-alternate Cai variants to the Guo backbone.

This representation is used as a controlled comparison genome:

- Guo scaffold structure is retained
- confident Cai fixed alleles are introduced
- assembly/reconstruction structure is therefore held largely constant
- sequence divergence can be examined separately from large-scale reconstruction differences

SeqKit summary statistics for the resulting pseudogenome are:

- sequences: **18,718**
- total length: **2,060,941,893 bp**
- N50: **251,615 bp**
- GC: **26.29%**
- Ns: **38,428,485**

The Cai-fixed representation is a derived analytical pseudogenome and must not be interpreted as an independently assembled Cai genome.

## RNA-seq mapping control

The same three Guo RNA-seq libraries were mapped to several genome representations.

Mean HISAT2 overall alignment rates were approximately:

- Guo: **96.92%**
- Cai-fixed: **96.79%**
- Cai Flye–HyPo: **95.88%**
- published Cai: **95.24%**
- Cai min1250: **95.15%**

The near-equivalence of Guo and Cai-fixed suggests that introducing confident Cai fixed alleles onto the Guo structural backbone has only a minor effect on transcriptomic mappability compared with the larger differences observed for independently reconstructed Cai genomes.

This result supports the use of Cai-fixed as a sequence-divergence control in later annotation comparisons.

## Relationship to later phases

Outputs from this phase feed directly into:

- **Phase 3 — harmonized comparison**
- **Phase 4 — repeat annotation**
- **Phase 5 — structural gene annotation**

In particular, Cai-fixed is used alongside Guo, Cai Flye–HyPo, and filtered published Cai to distinguish sequence-level divergence from reconstruction-associated effects.

## Interpretation limits

The Cai and Guo datasets represent two accessions, not population samples.

Accordingly:

- sequence differences should not be generalized as species-wide polymorphism;
- structural discrepancies should not automatically be called biological structural variants;
- unsupported Guo regions should not automatically be interpreted as accession-specific sequence;
- all major conclusions should distinguish biological variation from assembly- and reconstruction-associated effects.

## Current interpretation

The central result of this phase is that the large assembly-size difference between published Cai and Guo cannot be interpreted directly as biological genome-size divergence.

Independent Cai reads support most of the Guo assembly, while millions of small variants and tens of thousands of long-read structural discrepancies remain detectable. The Cai-fixed pseudogenome therefore provides a useful controlled representation for separating sequence divergence from genome reconstruction in subsequent analyses.
