# Track 90 — Andrian Exploratory Internship

**Status: EXPLORATORY ANALYSIS CLOSED — TRAINING / PROVENANCE ARCHIVE**

Track 90 records exploratory and supervised analyses performed during Andrian
Dary Fawwaz's RE-SAPRIA internship.

This track was designed as a training environment rather than as part of the
manuscript's analytical critical path. It allowed assembly, QC, troubleshooting,
parameter testing, and interpretation to be explored without silently mixing
experimental work with the controlled RE-SAPRIA pipeline.

The main RE-SAPRIA analysis reached final analytical freeze on **2026-10-04**.
No new exploratory results from Track 90 should now be introduced into the
current manuscript unless explicitly required to resolve an identified problem
and reproduced within the appropriate main analytical phase.

---

## Learning objective

The central training objective was to learn how to distinguish between:

- a computational job that finishes successfully;
- a numerically attractive result;
- a biologically credible result;
- a result supported by independent evidence;
- and a manuscript-grade reproducible analysis.

The unusually repeat-rich genome of *Sapria himalayana* provided a deliberately
difficult case study for learning these distinctions.

---

## Relationship to the main RE-SAPRIA analysis

Track 90 is **not an independent analytical phase**.

The manuscript-grade RE-SAPRIA record is contained in Phases 0–7.

Exploratory work in this directory may have informed troubleshooting,
interpretation, or training, but only analyses that were independently
standardized, reproduced, and incorporated into the main phase directories are
part of the frozen analytical record.

Therefore:

> **Track 90 documents the learning process; Phases 0–7 document the scientific evidence.**

---

## Final scientific context

RE-SAPRIA ultimately moved beyond a simple Cai-versus-Guo comparison.

The frozen analysis supports a broader picture of *Sapria himalayana*:

**extreme repeat abundance → reconstruction-sensitive genome representation →
repeat-expanded introns → repeat-sensitive gene prediction → comparatively
stable conserved protein-coding space.**

Track 90 should now be read in that final context. Early assembly experiments
were useful because they exposed how difficult repeat-rich plant genomes can be,
but individual exploratory assemblies or parameter combinations should not be
interpreted as competing final genome models.

## Post-freeze internship role

Following analytical freeze, the internship shifts from generating new genome
analyses toward learning scientific interpretation and manuscript development.

Appropriate post-freeze activities include:

- interpreting finalized figures and tables;
- explaining why individual analyses were performed;
- identifying limitations without generating unnecessary new analyses;
- drafting manuscript text from the frozen evidence;
- and learning how exploratory work becomes a reproducible scientific story.

New assembly or annotation experiments are outside the current manuscript scope
unless specifically required during manuscript revision or peer review.

---

## Preservation rule

Exploratory, unsuccessful, or superseded analyses should not be deleted merely
because they did not enter the manuscript.

When sufficiently documented, they remain useful as records of:

- troubleshooting;
- parameter sensitivity;
- computational limitations;
- analytical decision-making;
- and bioinformatics training.

They should, however, remain clearly separated from manuscript-grade outputs.
